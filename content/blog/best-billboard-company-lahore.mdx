## Does the company own its billboards, or resell them?

This is the first question, because it decides who fixes problems. A **media owner** builds and maintains its own structures. A **reseller** sells space on boards somebody else owns. When a light fails, a face tears in a storm or a mount runs late, an owner sends its own crew; a reseller has to ask another company and wait.

Resellers are not always a bad choice, because they can put together sites from several owners. But you should know which you are dealing with, and who is responsible when something goes wrong. ## Can you see the audience for each site before you pay?

A billboard's value is the number of people who pass it. A good company tells you that figure for every site, along with its size, its lighting and which traffic it faces, before you enquire. If a company can only offer "it's a very busy road", you have nothing to compare.

## Does it record which way each face points, and how it is lit?

A face is only seen by traffic driving towards it, and a non-lit board misses the evening commute in winter. A careful company records both for every site. Ask for the direction of approach and the illumination in writing: front-lit, back-lit, digital or non-lit.

## Who prints and mounts the face?

Outdoor campaigns most often go wrong in production: a print with the wrong colour, a face mounted days late, a flex that sags in the wind. Ask:

- Is the flex printed in-house or sent to a printer?
- Who mounts it, the company's own crew or a subcontractor?
- When is artwork due, and how long from approval to the face going up?

## Are permits handled?

Outdoor advertising in Lahore is regulated: structures and displays need approval from the PHA, and in cantonment areas from the cantonment authorities. A good company handles that paperwork for its own sites, so the advertiser never applies for it and never receives a takedown notice. Ask who is responsible if a face is removed.

## Will you get proof that every face ran?

This question separates good billboard companies from the rest. You should receive:

- A **dated photograph of every static face**, not one photo for the whole campaign.
- A **play log** for every digital screen, showing when each spot played.
- A clear rule for what happens if a face did not run: a make-good or no charge.

## Red flags when choosing a billboard company

- No audience figure, or the same figure for every site.
- No photographs of the actual face, only mock-ups.
- Vague answers about who owns the structure.
- One "campaign photo" instead of a photo of every face.
- No clear rule for what happens if a board does not go up.
- Pressure to book the cheapest sites, regardless of whether your audience passes them.

## Your shortlist checklist

Score every billboard company you are considering with these yes/no questions:

1. Do they own the structures, or tell you clearly who does?
2. Is the daily audience published for each site?
3. Is the direction of approach and lighting recorded for each face?
4. Do they print and mount with their own team?
5. Do they handle PHA and cantonment permits?
6. Will you receive a dated photograph of every face, and a play log for every screen?
7. Are the booking cycle and artwork deadline clear in writing?
8. Can they show real campaigns on their own boards?
9. Do their sites stand on the roads your customers drive?

The company with the most "yes" answers *and* the right roads is the best billboard company for you.
