Ask for a "standard billboard size" in Pakistan and you will not get one answer. Sizes are set by the structure and the plot or bridge it stands on, not by a national standard. What does exist is a set of recognisable *formats*, each with its own typical dimensions and its own job. This guide covers each one, using the real faces on Fangrove Media's Lahore network as the reference.

## Billboards and hoardings

The roadside billboard, also called a hoarding, is the main format: a large face on its own structure, usually lit from the front. On the network the largest is <Stat name="largestFace" /> at <Stat name="largestFaceSite" />.

Not every billboard is landscape. Some plots only allow a tall structure, which produces portrait faces such as 30 × 40 ft on Main Boulevard Gulberg or 15 × 45 ft on Link Road in Model Town. Portrait faces need their own artwork; a landscape design cropped to fit rarely works.

## Bridge and underpass panels

Panels mounted on bridges and underpasses are long and shallow, typically 50 × 10 ft on bridges and 40 × 10 ft in underpasses on this network. Traffic reads them head-on as it approaches, often while slowing for the bridge or the underpass, and on this network they carry some of the highest daily audience figures.

The proportions are unusual, at four or five to one, so artwork should be designed for the format: a short line of copy and a single strong image, not a squeezed version of a standard poster.

## Digital LED screens

Digital screens on the network come in two scales: a large roadside LED screen at a junction, and small digital streamers mounted in runs along a road. Screens are bought by slots in a loop rather than by the face. [DOOH vs static billboards](/blog/dooh-vs-static-billboards) explains how that works.

## Streamers, moppies and cutouts

The small formats are sold in runs rather than one at a time:

- **Streamers** are small panels, around 6 × 3 ft, placed in runs along a road or around a neighbourhood.
- **Moppies** are double-sided panels at street level, around 5 × 10 ft, common inside housing societies.
- **Cutouts** are shaped panels placed at major locations across an area.

They build frequency within a neighbourhood rather than impact from one face, and they are usually unlit.

Two points catch people out. First, the file is built at **1:10 scale**, so a 40 × 20 ft face is a 4 × 2 ft document. Second, large faces are wider than a single roll, so they are made of welded panels, and important text should not sit across a seam. ## Does a bigger face always perform better?

No. Size helps legibility from a distance, but location, direction of approach and lighting matter more. A mid-sized lit face on a slow, congested route will usually do more than a huge face on an open road where traffic passes at speed.
