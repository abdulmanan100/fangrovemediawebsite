Billboard advertising is worth it when the people you want to reach drive past the same place often, and when your message can be understood in a few seconds. It is a poor buy when you need an immediate click, when your audience is spread thinly across the country, or when the site has been chosen for its price rather than its traffic. Most disappointing billboard campaigns fail on the site, not on the medium.

This guide sets out what billboards do well, what they cannot do, and how to tell in advance which side of the line your campaign is on.

## What does billboard advertising do well?

### It reaches people who are not looking for you

Search ads reach people who already know what they want. Social ads reach people the platform has decided to show them to. A billboard reaches everyone on the road, including the people who did not know your brand existed until this morning.

### It builds frequency cheaply

Commuters use the same roads twice a day, five or six days a week. A single well-placed face is seen by many of the same people again and again over a month, and repetition is what makes a message stick. On Fangrove Media's network, every site publishes the daily audience it carries, from the smaller streamer and moppie runs up to the busiest corridors.

### It cannot be skipped or blocked

There is no skip button on a billboard and no ad blocker for a bridge panel. The message stays up around the clock for the whole booking, and on a lit face it keeps working through the evening commute.

### It makes a brand look established

A brand on a large face on a main road reads as a serious business. For new entrants, housing schemes, schools, hospitals and retailers opening a new branch, that signal is often the point.

## What can't a billboard do?

Be honest about the limits before you book:

- **No click.** Nobody can tap a billboard. Response comes later, by search, phone, WhatsApp or a visit, so it has to be measured differently. [How to measure a billboard campaign](/blog/measure-billboard-campaign) covers how.
- **A short message.** A driver has seconds, not minutes. A billboard carries one idea well and three ideas badly.
- **Lead time.** A static face needs artwork 72 hours before the mount and runs in monthly cycles. If the message changes daily, a [digital screen](/services/dooh) suits it better.
- **Location risk.** A busy road is no use if the face points the wrong way or stands behind a tree. The site decides the result.

## Who is billboard advertising best for?

Businesses whose customers live or travel in a defined part of the city get the most from it: retailers, malls, restaurants, car showrooms, real estate, schools and universities, hospitals and clinics. National brands use billboards in a different way, as the frequency layer under television and digital, often around a launch or a seasonal sale.

## How do you make sure a billboard is worth the money?

Most of the risk can be removed before you pay:

1. **Ask for the audience figure for each site.** If a seller cannot tell you how many people pass the face each day, you cannot compare it to anything. Fangrove Media publishes the figure on every [site page](/network).
2. **Check the direction of approach.** A face is only seen by traffic driving towards it. Each Fangrove site records which traffic it faces.
3. **Check the lighting.** In winter a non-lit face misses the evening commute. Front-lit and back-lit faces keep working after dark.
4. **Hold the corridor long enough.** Outdoor works through repetition. One cycle on one face rarely shows what the medium can do.
5. **Insist on proof.** A dated photograph of every static face and a play log for every screen. If it did not run, you should not pay for it.
