An outdoor media campaign is more than a booking for one billboard. It is a set of faces, and often digital screens, radio or press alongside them, chosen so that the same people see the message often enough to act on it. This is how Fangrove Media plans one in Lahore, from the brief to the proof that every face ran.

## Start with the audience, not the board

Write down who you need to reach and where they travel. A campaign for a DHA showroom and one for a Ferozepur Road wholesaler should not share a site list. Outdoor works through **repetition**: commuter routes carry the same people twice a day, five or six days a week, so the strongest plans pick a few corridors and hold them rather than scattering single faces across the city.

## Choose the mix of formats

Each outdoor format does a different job in a campaign:

<FormatTable />

- **Billboards and hoardings** own a face around the clock for a month. They carry the main message and build frequency.
- **Bridge panels and underpass faces** sit directly in the driver's line of sight and are hard to miss.
- **Digital screens (DOOH)** sell slots in a loop and let the message change by time of day, which suits offers, launches and countdowns. [DOOH vs static billboards](/blog/dooh-vs-static-billboards) compares the two.
- **Streamers** are sold as a run of many faces along one road or around one neighbourhood, so a single trip passes the message again and again.

Most campaigns use a mix: large static faces to own the corridor, with digital screens where the message needs to change.

## Add other media where it earns its place

Outdoor rarely needs to work alone. Radio is a cheap way to build frequency with a drive-time audience, and it reaches the same commute your billboards stand on. Television and press add national reach when the campaign runs beyond Lahore. Fangrove Media plans and buys campaigns across Lahore, Islamabad and Karachi, with broadcast and national press across Pakistan, so all of it can sit in one [media campaign](/services/media-campaigns) plan. [What is a 360 media campaign](/blog/360-media-campaign-pakistan) explains how the media fit together.

The opposite is also true: some briefs are best met with two well-placed billboards and nothing else. A smaller correct plan is worth more than a large wrong one.

## Set the dates and the length

Static sites are booked by the month, in cycles that start on the **1st or the 16th**. Pick the cycle, then work backwards: final artwork is due **72 hours before the mount**, and digital creative goes live within four working hours of approval. Longer bookings are priced differently from a single month, so decide on the length before asking for rates. ## Measure what ran

A campaign is only as good as the proof that it ran. On Fangrove Media campaigns that means a dated photograph of every static face, a weekly play log for each digital screen, tear sheets for press and transmission reconciliation for broadcast. If a placement did not run, you do not pay for it. [How to measure a billboard campaign](/blog/measure-billboard-campaign) covers proof and response in detail, and [billboard campaign examples from Lahore](/blog/billboard-campaign-examples-lahore) shows real campaigns on these roads.

<Callout title="A useful rule of thumb">

Choose two or three corridors your audience actually drives, hold them for at least a full cycle, and add other media only where it reaches the same people at a different moment.
