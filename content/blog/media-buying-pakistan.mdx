Media buying is the work of securing advertising space and airtime at the right price, in the right position, and then proving it ran. In Pakistan each medium is bought differently: billboards by the monthly cycle, digital screens by the slot, television and radio by the time belt, and newspapers by the insertion and the page position. A media buyer's value lies in knowing those rules, holding the relationships that get good positions, and checking every placement afterwards.

This guide explains how each medium is bought in Pakistan, what to ask a media buying agency, and where money usually leaks out of a budget.

## What is the difference between media planning and media buying?

**Planning** decides what to buy: which media, which sites or channels, how much weight and when. **Buying** turns that plan into booked placements: negotiating rates and positions, issuing the bookings, and reconciling what ran against what was paid for. The two work best together, because a plan that cannot be bought at the assumed price is only a wish. [What is a 360 media campaign](/blog/360-media-campaign-pakistan) covers the planning side.

## How are TV and radio bought?

Broadcast is planned by **reach and frequency** and bought by **belt**: the time band a spot airs in, such as prime time. Plans are built from ratings data, and airtime is negotiated directly with channels, often with bonus weight or sponsorship idents included.

The step that separates good broadcast buying from bad is **log reconciliation**: checking every spot against the channel's transmission log. If a spot did not run in the belt it was bought in, it should be recovered as a make-good rather than written off. That reconciliation is where a lot of value quietly leaks out of Pakistani media budgets. Television commercials also need PEMRA content clearance before they air.

Radio is bought the same way and is the cheapest frequency-builder for a drive-time audience. It pairs naturally with outdoor on the same commute.

## How is newspaper advertising bought?

Press is bought by the insertion: which title, which edition, which size and which page position. Position is negotiated, not left to chance: right-hand page, above the fold, facing editorial and away from the classifieds are the details that separate a noticed advertisement from a paid-for one. Edition-level buying means a Lahore campaign does not pay for Karachi circulation.

## What should you ask a media buying agency?

1. **Do you own any of the inventory, or resell it?** Ownership changes who fixes a problem.
2. **Will I see the plan line by line?** Each placement, its cost and its expected audience, not a single total.
3. **What are the assumptions behind the audience figures?** Published site audiences, ratings, circulation.
4. **What proof will I receive for each medium?** Photographs, play logs, transmission logs, tear sheets.
5. **What happens if something does not run?** The answer should be a make-good or no charge, in writing.

## Where does money leak out of media budgets?

- **Spots that did not air in the bought belt** and were never reconciled.
- **Boards that were not lit**, were torn, or went up late, with no dated photograph to show it.
- **Screens with crowded loops**, where more advertisers were added than disclosed.
- **Press placed wherever the desk had space**, rather than in the position paid for.
- **Duplicated reach**, where two media are bought to do the same job.

Each one is preventable with a line-by-line plan and proof for every placement.
