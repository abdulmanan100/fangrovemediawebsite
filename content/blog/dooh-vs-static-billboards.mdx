Digital out-of-home (DOOH) and static billboards sit on the same roads and are often sold by the same company, but they are different purchases. A static billboard rents you a whole face for a month. A digital screen sells you a recurring slot in a loop that other advertisers share. Which one is right depends far less on budget than on what the campaign has to do.

## What is the difference between DOOH and a static billboard?

| | Static billboard | Digital screen (DOOH) |
|---|---|---|
| What you buy | The whole face, every hour of the day | A slot in a shared loop |
| Booking unit | Monthly cycles from the 1st or 16th | Share of voice in the loop |
| Changing creative | A new flex print and a remount | A new file, live within hours |
| Messages at once | One | Several, by time of day if you want |
| Proof it ran | Dated photograph of the face | Timestamped play log |
| Production | Flex printed and mounted overnight | MP4 or JPG at native resolution |

## When should you choose a static billboard?

Choose static when one message needs to *own* a location:

- **Brand presence on a single corridor.** A static face is yours around the clock for the whole cycle, with no other advertiser in between.
- **Large faces.** The biggest formats are static. The largest face on the network is <Stat name="largestFace" />, far larger than any screen.
- **Long, stable campaigns.** If the creative will not change for months, paying for the flexibility of a screen buys you nothing.
- **Areas without screens.** Most of the network is static, so for many corridors it is the only option.

## When should you choose DOOH?

Choose digital when the message has to move:

- **Time-sensitive offers.** Sales, launches, events and countdowns, where the creative changes within days.
- **Dayparting.** Different creative for the morning commute, midday and the evening peak, booked as one line item.
- **Several products.** A rotation of variants on one screen, rather than one product per printed face.
- **Regulated or sensitive categories.** A spot can be pulled or replaced within two hours if it has to be.
- **Proof of delivery.** A timestamped play log for every spot, weekly.

## Can you combine the two?

Often that is the strongest plan. A static face holds the brand on a corridor for the month, while a screen on the same route carries the changing message: this week's offer, today's event. Because both are planned, printed and verified by one team, they can be booked as one campaign. The [how to book a billboard](/blog/how-to-book-a-billboard-lahore) guide covers the steps for both.

<Callout title="A common mistake">

Buying a screen and then running one creative, unchanged, for a month. That gives up the screen's advantages while still sharing the loop with other advertisers. If the creative will not change, a static face is usually the better buy.

</Callout>
