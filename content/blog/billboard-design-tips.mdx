A good billboard design can be understood by a driver in a single glance. That means one message, very few words, a large brand name, and strong contrast that still works under the face's lighting at night. Most weak billboards are not ugly; they are crowded. They were designed on a screen held at arm's length, then printed many feet wide and read from across a junction.

This guide covers how to design billboard artwork that works on a Lahore road, and how to set up the file so it prints correctly the first time.

## How long does someone look at a billboard?

Only a few seconds, and often while doing something else. How long depends on the site: traffic slowing at a roundabout or a signal gives a face a full read, while traffic moving fast on an open road gives it a glance. That is why Fangrove Media records each site's direction of approach and notes where dwell time is long. Hussain Chowk in Gulberg is one example: roundabout traffic means a full read rather than a glance.

Design for the worst case. If the message works in a glance, it will also work in a full read.

## What makes a billboard design work?

### One idea

A billboard carries one message well and three badly. Decide the single thing the driver should remember, and cut everything else: secondary offers, lists of branches, full addresses, small print.

### Few words

Short copy is not a style choice on a billboard; it is a reading-time limit. A headline of a few words and a brand name is usually enough. If the message needs a paragraph, it belongs in [press](/services/print-media) or on a landing page.

### A brand you can see from the road

The logo or brand name should be large enough to read at the distance the site is viewed from. A beautiful image with a small logo in the corner advertises the category, not the brand.

### One image, big

A single strong photograph or illustration, cropped tight, reads faster than a collage. Products should be large enough to recognise at speed.

### Contrast that survives the lighting

A face is seen in daylight and, if lit, at night. Fine tonal differences that work on a monitor flatten out on flex at a distance. Use strong contrast between text and background, and avoid thin type and text laid over busy parts of a photograph.

## Does the lighting change the design?

Yes. Front-lit and back-lit faces are not the same job. A front-lit face is lit by lamps shining onto the print; a back-lit face glows from behind, so colours and dark areas behave differently. Fangrove Media proofs colour against the illumination the face will actually live under, and [the star flex printing guide](/blog/star-flex-printing-guide) explains which flex suits which lighting.

Non-lit faces need extra care in winter, when darkness falls before the evening commute. If the board must work after dark, choose a lit site.

## What about the format of the face?

Faces come in different shapes, from wide landscape boards to tall portrait faces on Main Boulevard and MM Alam Road. Design for the actual site, not a generic billboard ratio: a layout built for a wide face will not survive being squeezed onto a portrait one. Every site's dimensions are on its page on the [network map](/network), and [billboard sizes in Pakistan](/blog/billboard-sizes-pakistan) lists them by format.

If you are booking several sizes in one campaign, design a master layout and adapt it to each face rather than stretching one file.

## How should billboard artwork files be set up?

These are the specifications Fangrove Media prints to on its own flex floor:

- **Scale:** build the file at 1:10 scale.
- **Colour:** CMYK, not RGB.
- **Resolution:** 150 dpi at that 1:10 scale.
- **Deadline:** final artwork 72 hours before the mount.

Files are checked against the face's size and lighting before anything is printed, so a problem is caught while there is still time to fix it. ## A checklist before you send the file

1. Can a stranger say what is being advertised after one glance?
2. Is the brand name readable at the distance of the site?
3. Is there only one message?
4. Does the text still stand out if the face is viewed at night?
5. Is the file built for this face's exact proportions?
6. Is it CMYK, 1:10 scale, 150 dpi at scale?

<Callout title="The rule that matters most">

Design for a driver who sees the board for a moment while thinking about something else. If it works for them, it works for everyone.
