Most billboard print problems are not printing problems. They come from choosing the wrong material for the structure, setting up the file for a monitor instead of a road, or approving colour under the wrong light. This guide covers the decisions that matter, using the specification of Fangrove Media's own large-format floor, which prints the faces its crews mount.

## What is star flex?

Star flex is the PVC-coated fabric most Pakistani billboards are printed on. People also call it panaflex or simply flex. It comes in different weights, measured in grams per square metre (gsm). Heavier flex is stronger and holds its shape better under wind, at the cost of weight on the structure.

## Which gsm should you use?

Billboard flex on Fangrove Media's sites is **440 to 650 gsm star flex**. The weight is chosen per site:

- **Heavier flex** where the structure takes real wind load: tall, exposed faces and wide spans.
- **Lighter flex** where the face is smaller or sheltered.

The material is welded and eyeleted to suit the structure's rigging, which is one reason the same team handling print and mounting saves trouble.

## Front-lit, backlit, mesh or vinyl?

The right substrate depends on how the face is lit and where it is mounted:

| Material | Use it for | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Front-lit flex | Billboards lit by lamps aimed at the face | Opaque, so it reflects the lamps' light |
| Backlit flex | Boxes and panels lit from inside | Translucent stock printed at higher ink density so colour survives the light behind it |
| Mesh | Building wraps and exposed structures | Perforated, so wind passes through instead of loading the structure |
| Vinyl and one-way vision | Vehicles, glass, floors and curved fascia | Self-adhesive, and it follows curves |

Almost every billboard on Fangrove Media's network is front-lit. Backlit flex is for lightboxes, shelters and backlit underpass panels.

In practice:

- **Work at 1:10 scale.** A 40 × 20 ft face is a 4 × 2 ft document at 150 dpi. Full-size files at high resolution are slower to handle and look no sharper from the road.
- **Supply CMYK.** RGB colours that look vivid on screen can shift when converted at print.
- **Keep text large and short.** If it cannot be read at a glance from a moving car, it will not be read.
- **Mind the seams.** Faces wider than the roll are welded from panels. Keep faces, logos and key text away from the joins.

## Why does billboard colour look different from the proof?

The most common failure is colour approved on a bright monitor and then hung on a front-lit face. A red that looks vivid on screen can turn muddy under street lighting at night. The fix is to proof against the light the face will actually live under, which is why front-lit and backlit are treated as different jobs from the first proof.

## How long does billboard flex last?

Fangrove Media prints with eco-solvent and solvent inks rated **UV-stable for 24 months**.

## How fast can flex be printed and mounted?

Printing takes about **24 hours**, and mounting can follow the same night. For a booked site, artwork is due 72 hours before the mount so it can be checked against the face size and lighting first. Crews mount overnight, between 11 p.m. and 5 a.m., so the board is never half-dressed during traffic.
