A 360 media campaign puts one message across several media at once, typically billboards and digital screens outdoors, television and radio on air, newspapers in print and digital channels online, so the same audience meets it in different places through the day. In Pakistan the term usually means a campaign built around a launch or a season, with outdoor as the always-on layer and broadcast or press adding reach and authority around it.

The point is not to use every medium. It is to use each medium for the job it does best, and plan them against each other so they add up.

## What does each medium do in a 360 campaign?

Each medium has a different strength, and a good plan gives each one a job:

| Medium | What it does best |
|---|---|
| Billboards and hoardings | Frequency on the routes the audience travels, around the clock |
| Digital screens (DOOH) | Messages that change by time of day, and fast creative swaps |
| Television | Broad reach and emotional storytelling, across the country |
| Radio | Cheap frequency with a drive-time audience, on the same commute as outdoor |
| Cinema | A captive audience in multiplexes, around film releases |
| Newspapers | Authority with decision-makers, and statutory or corporate notices |

Outdoor and radio pair naturally: the same driver hears the spot and passes the board on the same journey. Television brings reach outdoor cannot match outside the cities you have faces in. Press reaches people who sign things: procurement officers, contractors and officials.

## When do you need a 360 campaign?

A multi-media plan earns its cost when:

- **You are launching** a product, a branch or a brand and need to be seen everywhere at once for a short period.
- **The audience is national**, so outdoor in one city cannot reach it alone.
- **The decision is long**, such as property, cars, education or insurance, and buyers need to meet the brand several times in different settings.
- **There is a date to hit**, like a sale, an admission deadline or an event, and every medium has to point at it.

It is the wrong answer when the audience lives in one part of one city and the message is simple. Then two or three well-chosen billboards may do more than a thin spread across six media. A smaller correct plan is worth more than a large wrong one.

## How do you plan a 360 media campaign?

### 1. Start from the audience and the date

Who must be reached, where, and by when? Everything else follows from those three answers.

### 2. Give each medium one job

Decide which medium builds frequency, which builds reach, which carries detail and which drives the response. If two media do the same job, cut one.

### 3. Sequence the media

Media should not all start on the same morning by accident. A typical sequence uses press or television to announce, outdoor to repeat through the period, digital screens to switch to countdown or offer messages as the date approaches, and radio to reach the commute in the final days.

### 4. Cost it line by line

Every placement should appear in the plan with its cost and the audience it is expected to deliver. You should see the impression model and the assumptions behind it, not a total at the bottom of a page.

### 5. Keep the creative consistent

One idea, adapted to each medium: a few words on the billboard, a short loop on the screen, a story on television, detail in the press advertisement. The audience should recognise the same campaign wherever they meet it.

## How do you know a 360 campaign actually ran?

Each medium has its own proof. Ask for all of it:

- A dated photograph of every static face.
- A weekly play log for every digital screen.
- Transmission log reconciliation for every TV and radio spot, with missed spots recovered as make-goods.
- A scanned tear sheet for every press insertion.
