SMD screen advertising means buying airtime on an outdoor LED screen: the large digital displays at Lahore's busiest junctions that show a rotating loop of advertisers. "SMD" refers to the surface-mounted LEDs the screens are built from, and in Lahore it has become the everyday name for any roadside digital billboard. You do not rent the whole screen. You buy a slot in its loop, and your message plays every time the loop comes round.

This guide covers how SMD screen advertising is bought, what to supply, and how to get more from a screen than a static billboard can give you. If you are still deciding between a screen and a printed face, start with [DOOH vs static billboards](/blog/dooh-vs-static-billboards).

## What is pixel pitch, and why does it matter?

Pixel pitch is the distance between the centres of two neighbouring pixels, in millimetres. A smaller number means pixels sit closer together, so the image looks sharp from nearer. Fangrove Media's outdoor screens are <Spec service="dooh" label="Pixel pitch" />: pitches made for roadside viewing, where the audience is metres away, not centimetres. Screens are calibrated for legibility in daylight and dimmed after dusk so they stay readable without glare.

For the advertiser, the practical point is simple: design for the distance. Fine print that looks crisp on a laptop disappears on a roadside screen.

## What should SMD screen creative look like?

A slot is short, so treat it as a moving billboard rather than a TV commercial:

- **One message per slot.** The viewer may catch only part of a play. Make every second of it make sense on its own.
- **Large type, high contrast.** A brand name and one line of copy, big enough to read from across the junction.
- **Native resolution.** Supply the file at the screen's own resolution so nothing is scaled or cropped. Fangrove Media takes <Spec service="dooh" label="Creative format" />.
- **No sound.** Roadside screens are silent. If the message needs a voice-over, it belongs on [radio](/services/electronic-media).

## What can a screen do that a printed billboard can't?

This is where SMD screens earn their place:

- **Dayparting.** Run one message for the morning commute, another at midday and another at evening peak, booked as one line item.
- **Dynamic creative.** Copy driven by a feed: weather, price, stock, a fixture or a countdown. Built once, it runs unattended.
- **Fast changes.** New creative goes live within four working hours of approval, and can be pulled or replaced within two hours in an emergency. That is why regulated categories and fast-moving offers choose digital.
- **Several variants.** Rotate a range of products or offers through the same slot instead of choosing one.

## How do you know your ad actually played?

You get a play log: timestamped proof of every spot, delivered weekly, with a monthly impression summary and photographic verification on request. That is a stronger proof of delivery than most media offer, and it is the basis for checking what you paid for. [How to measure a billboard campaign](/blog/measure-billboard-campaign) covers what to do with it.

## How do you book an SMD screen in Lahore?

The enquiry is the same as for a billboard: name the screen (or just the area), the start date and how long you want to run. With digital you also choose how many slots and whether you want dayparting. There is no printing or mounting step, so a screen campaign can start far sooner than a static one.
