This guide explains the kinds of media and advertising companies in Lahore, what to ask each one, and the warning signs. Fangrove Media's own answers are shown where useful, so you can see what a clear answer looks like and hold us to the same standard.

## What kinds of media company are there?

Before comparing companies, know what kind each one is:

- **Creative agencies** make the advertising: the idea, the design, the commercial. Some also buy media; many pass it to someone else.
- **Media agencies** (media planning and buying agencies) decide where the advertising goes and buy the space and airtime.
- **Media owners** own the space itself: billboards, screens, channels or newspapers.

Some companies combine roles. Fangrove Media is a media owner and a media agency in one: it owns <Stat name="totalSites" /> outdoor sites in Lahore, and plans and buys TV, radio, cinema and press across Pakistan. It also produces print and broadcast work. Knowing the mix tells you where a company's interests lie: an owner will naturally favour its own sites, which is why the plan should be open to inspection.

## Which media can they actually buy?

Ask for the list, and ask which ones are bought directly rather than through someone else. A full media campaign in Pakistan can include:

| Medium | What a good company should offer |
|---|---|
| Billboards and outdoor | Published audience per site, own or named structures, proof of every face |
| Digital screens (DOOH) | Loop length, slot length and advertiser count stated up front, play logs |
| Television | Ratings-led plans, direct channel buying, PEMRA clearance, log reconciliation |
| Radio | Drive-time planning, station buying, transmission proof |
| Cinema | Multiplex pre-roll bought against confirmed release slates |
| Newspapers | Edition-level buying, negotiated positions, tear sheets |

## Will they show you the plan line by line?

A good media company does not hand you a total at the bottom of a page. It shows each placement, what it costs, and the audience it is expected to deliver, with the impression model and the assumptions behind it. If the plan cannot be read line by line, it cannot be checked.

## Do they start by asking what your last campaign returned?

The best media advice often begins with stopping something. A company worth hiring asks what your previous campaigns cost and what they returned before proposing anything new, and is willing to say no to the wrong buy. Fangrove Media has talked clients out of digital screens, off television and down to two billboards, because a smaller correct invoice is worth more than a large wrong one.

## How will they prove the media ran?

Each medium has its own proof, and a good agency supplies all of it:

- **Outdoor:** a dated photograph of every face.
- **Digital screens:** a timestamped play log.
- **Television and radio:** reconciliation against the transmission log, with missed spots recovered as make-goods.
- **Press:** a tear sheet for every insertion.

On Fangrove Media campaigns, these arrive as a monthly verification pack, and if a placement did not run, you do not pay for it. [How to measure a billboard campaign](/blog/measure-billboard-campaign) covers turning proof into results.

## Red flags when choosing a media agency

- A plan with a single total and no line items.
- Audience or reach figures with no source.
- No mention of proof until you ask for it.
- Every medium recommended, whatever the brief.
- No answer to "what happens if a spot doesn't run?"
- Vague about which inventory they own and which they resell.

## Your shortlist checklist

Score every media or advertising agency you are considering:

1. Do they explain clearly whether they are a creative agency, a media agency, a media owner or a mix?
2. Can they buy every medium your brief needs, and say which they buy directly?
3. Will they show the plan line by line, with the assumptions behind the audience figures?
4. Did they ask what your last campaign cost and returned?
5. Will you receive proof for every placement in every medium?
6. Is there a written rule for placements that did not run?
7. Do they cover the cities you need?
8. Is production in-house, or clearly managed?
9. Can they show real campaigns for brands like yours?
